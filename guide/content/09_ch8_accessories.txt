# [Chapter 8] Shoes, Belts, Watches, Hats, Glasses and More

LEAD: Accessories are where people look second, after your face. They are small, inexpensive to get right, and they say a great deal about how carefully a man looks after himself.

## Shoes

### The three shoes most men need

| W=1.7,2.3,2 | Shoe | Wear it with | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leather lace-ups (plain or toe-cap) | Jackets, suits, wool trousers, chinos | Brown for everyday; black or very dark brown for formal |
| Casual leather or suede shoes | Chinos, jeans, corduroys, knitwear | Brown, tan, navy suede |
| Clean leather sneakers | Casual outfits only | White, off-white, navy or grey; plain, no large logos |

### Shoe fit checks

[ ] A thumb's width of space beyond your longest toe
[ ] Heel does not slip more than a little as you walk
[ ] The widest part of your foot sits at the widest part of the shoe
[ ] Try shoes on in the afternoon, when feet are slightly larger, wearing the socks you will wear
[ ] Walk on a hard floor, not just the shop carpet

### Signs a shoe needs attention

| W=2.3,3.7 | Sign | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Heel worn down on one side | Cobbler: new heel. Cheap and quick |
| Sole worn thin or holed | Cobbler: resole (on good leather shoes) |
| Grey, dull, scuffed leather | Clean and polish (Chapter 9) |
| Cracks across the toe crease | Condition the leather; if deep, replace |
| Laces frayed | Replace laces: a tiny cost for a big difference |
| Shoe has lost its shape | Use shoe trees; if collapsed, replace |

!TIP Cedar shoe trees are one of the best investments you can make. They keep shoes in shape, absorb moisture and make leather shoes last years longer.

!WARN Sneakers with suits: some younger men do it. It rarely works on men over 60 and usually looks like you forgot your shoes. Keep leather sneakers for casual outfits.

## Socks

- Match socks to your **trousers**, not your shoes. Grey trousers, grey socks; navy trousers, navy socks.
- Socks should be long enough that no bare leg shows when you sit down and cross your legs.
- Avoid white athletic socks with anything other than sports clothing.
- A little colour (burgundy, a subtle pattern) is fine with smart-casual outfits.

## Belts

- **Match the belt to your shoes:** brown with brown, black with black. This is one of the few firm rules.
- **Width:** about 1.25 inches (3–3.5 cm) for smart trousers. Wider belts are for jeans.
- **Fit:** the buckle should fasten in the middle hole, with the end of the belt reaching just past the first belt loop.
- **Buckle:** plain and simple in silver or brass. Large or novelty buckles look dated.
- **Condition:** a belt cracked at the holes or stretched into an oval at the usual hole should be replaced.

!NOTE If you need to pull your belt very tight to keep trousers up, the trousers do not fit at the waist. Fix the trousers (Chapter 2) instead of punishing the belt.

## Watches

- A simple watch with a clear face is the most elegant choice at any age: easy to read, timeless.
- **Leather strap** (brown or black) for smart and smart-casual. **Metal bracelet** for everyday.
- The watch should sit just above the wrist bone, under the shirt cuff, and slide out when you bend your arm.
- Large, heavy, very sporty watches look out of place with a suit.
- If you wear a fitness or health tracker, a plain strap in navy, black or brown blends in best.

## Ties

- **Length:** the tip of the tie should reach the middle of the belt buckle. No higher, no lower.
- **Width:** the widest part of the tie should be close to the width of your jacket lapels.
- **Knot:** a simple four-in-hand or half-Windsor knot. Make it symmetrical and snug against the collar.
- **The dimple:** a small dip just under the knot looks deliberate and finished.
- **Colours for silver hair:** navy, burgundy, slate blue, grey, soft green, dusty pink. Small, regular patterns age well.

## Hats

- A hat is practical and can be very smart, especially for men with thinning hair in sun or cold.
- **Flat cap** in tweed or wool: casual and smart-casual.
- **Brimmed felt or straw hat:** smart-casual and travel. The brim should be in proportion to your shoulders.
- **Knit beanie:** casual and walking only.
- Take hats off indoors, at the table and in church, unless your faith asks you to cover your head.

## Glasses

Glasses sit in the centre of your face, so they influence your look more than any other accessory.

- **Frame shape:** choose frames that contrast with your face shape. Round faces suit more angular frames; square faces suit rounder frames.
- **Width:** the frame should be roughly as wide as the widest part of your face, with your eyes near the centre of each lens.
- **Colour:** tortoiseshell, warm brown, gunmetal, navy and soft grey flatter silver hair. Very heavy black frames can look harsh.
- **Brow line:** the top of the frame should follow, or sit just below, your eyebrows.
- **Update:** frames from more than ten years ago can date a face more than any garment. When your prescription changes, look again at the frame.

!TIP Take a trusted, honest person with you when choosing frames, and take phone photos of yourself in each pair. Glasses are surprisingly hard to judge in a small shop mirror without your own glasses on.

## Scarves and gloves

- A wool scarf in navy, grey, camel or burgundy adds warmth and colour near the face. Fold it in half and loop it through for a neat look.
- Leather gloves (brown or black) for smart coats; knitted gloves for casual jackets.

## Pocket squares

- A simple white cotton or linen square folded flat, showing about half an inch above the pocket, is always correct.
- It should complement the tie, not match it exactly.
- Keep the jacket breast pocket for the square only, not for pens or glasses.

## Bags and carrying things

Stuffed pockets ruin the line of a jacket (mistake number five).

- Carry a slim wallet in the inside jacket pocket only.
- Keys and phone go in a coat pocket, a trouser pocket, or a small bag.
- A simple leather or canvas messenger bag or a small bag that fastens at the waist under a coat is far smarter than bulging pockets.

## Grooming: the finishing accessory

- **Haircut** every four to six weeks keeps silver hair looking deliberate, not neglected.
- **Eyebrows, ears and nose:** ask your barber to trim these at every visit. It is routine for them.
- **Facial hair:** clean-shaven, or a beard trimmed and shaped regularly. Stubble between shaves looks tired after 60.
- **Hands and nails:** clean and trimmed. People notice hands when you shake them.
- **Scent:** one light spray, or none. Not three.

!TRY This week: polish your shoes, check your belt, and look at your glasses frames in a photo. Those three changes alone will make you look more put-together by Sunday.
