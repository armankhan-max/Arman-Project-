# [Chapter 3] Colours for Silver and Grey Hair

LEAD: As hair turns silver and skin tone softens, the colours that suited you at 35 may not suit you at 70. This is good news: the new palette is easier to wear, easier to combine, and makes you look healthier and warmer.

## What changes, and why it matters

With age, hair loses its pigment and the contrast between hair, eyebrows and skin softens. Skin often becomes lighter or slightly more uneven in tone. The result is a softer overall look.

Strong, harsh colours worn close to the face can now overpower you. The eye goes to the colour, not the man. Softer, deeper and warmer shades work *with* silver hair: they bring colour back into the face instead of draining it.

!WHY People mostly look at your face. Whatever you wear on your top half, especially at the collar, acts like a reflector. The right colour makes you look rested and well. The wrong one makes you look tired, even after a good night's sleep.

## The five-second chin test

This is the most useful colour test you will ever do.

1. Stand by a window in daylight with no other bright colours near you.
2. Hold a garment just under your chin, so the colour fills the space below your face.
3. Look at your face, not the garment. Does your skin look clearer, warmer, more even? Or greyer, more tired, with shadows under the eyes?
4. Swap to a second colour and compare.
5. Do this with five or six garments from your wardrobe. Sort them into *flatters me* and *does not*.

!TIP Ask your wife, daughter or a friend to watch while you do this. Other people see the difference faster than you do, because they are not distracted by the garment.

## Colours to handle carefully near the face

| W=1.4,2.6,2.4 | Colour | The problem | Better choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black | Drains colour, deepens shadows and lines | Navy, charcoal |
| Stark brilliant white | Can make skin look sallow or ruddy | Soft white, ivory, pale blue |
| Neon and very bright colours | Overpower soft colouring | The deeper version of the same colour |
| Mustard and yellow-green | Can make the skin look tired or sickly | Camel, olive |
| Beige too close to your skin tone | You disappear into it | Stone with a navy jacket over it |
| Bright orange | Clashes with ruddy or pink skin | Rust, brown |

!NOTE "Handle carefully" does not mean "never wear". Black trousers or black shoes are perfectly fine. The rule is about colour *next to the face*. If you love black, keep it for the evening and wear a lighter shirt under it.

## Contrast: the secret most men miss

Contrast is the difference between your lightest and darkest colours. Silver hair means your natural contrast is now lower.

- **Low-to-medium contrast outfits look best:** navy jacket, light blue shirt, grey trousers. Charcoal sweater over a pale blue shirt.
- **Very high contrast**, such as black with stark white, can look severe and make the face look washed out.
- **All one pale colour** can look washed out too. Add one deeper piece, usually the jacket or trousers.

## Patterns: a simple rule

- **One pattern per outfit** is always safe: a checked jacket with plain shirt and trousers, or a patterned tie with a plain shirt.
- **Two patterns** work when they are different in size: a small-check shirt with a large-check jacket.
- Avoid very small, tight patterns (fine herringbone in a busy colour, tiny checks) in strong contrast: they can shimmer on screens and in photographs.
- Subtle textures such as tweed, flannel and knit add interest without risk.
