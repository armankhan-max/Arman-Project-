# [Chapter 5] Smart Shopping

LEAD: Most wardrobe money is wasted in the shop, not the closet. A clear list, a fitting-room routine and a few warning signs will save you more than any sale.

## The buying mindset

- **Buy for the man you are today.** Not for the weight you hope to reach, and not for the man you were at 50.
- **Buy for the shoulders, alter the rest.** The shoulder decides whether a jacket is worth buying.
- **Fewer, better.** One jacket that fits and goes with five things beats three that sort of fit and go with one.
- **Budget for the tailor.** When you set a budget for a jacket or trousers, keep some of it back for alterations. The garment is not finished until it fits.

!WHY Shopping fatigue is real. After forty-five minutes in a shop, most people's decisions get worse and they buy just to be finished. Shop with a list, early in the day, and stop when you are tired.

## Your priority list

If your wardrobe needs work, buy in this order. Each step builds on the one before.

| W=0.4,2.3,3.3 | # | Buy | Why first? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trousers that fit at the waist | Everything else sits on top of them |
| 2 | Two pale blue shirts in your correct collar size | Instant improvement near the face |
| 3 | A navy jacket with the right shoulders | Goes with almost everything you own |
| 4 | A good pair of brown leather shoes | Lifts every outfit |
| 5 | A navy and a grey sweater | Comfort and colour near the face |
| 6 | A warm overcoat that fits over the jacket | Often the most visible garment in winter |
| 7 | A navy or charcoal suit | For the occasions that matter |
| 8 | Accessories: belt, ties, scarf | The finishing touches |

## Before you leave home

[ ] Know your current measurements (see the size sheet in Chapter 10). Re-measure every year or two.
[ ] Write down the specific item you need: "mid-grey trousers, higher rise, straight leg".
[ ] Wear or bring the shoes you will wear with the item.
[ ] Wear a collared shirt, so you can judge jackets properly.
[ ] Wear clothes that are easy to take off and on.
[ ] Take this guide, or a photo of the checklist below on your phone.
[ ] Decide your maximum budget, *including* alterations.
[ ] Go at a quiet time: weekday mornings are best.

## The fitting-room checklist

Take this into the fitting room. Do the checks in this order: if an item fails an early check, stop and try the next one.

### Jackets

[ ] Shoulder seam ends at my shoulder bone. (If not, stop here.)
[ ] No dent at the top of the sleeve.
[ ] Collar lies against my shirt collar at the back.
[ ] Buttoned: no X-pull, front lies flat.
[ ] I can reach forward and lift my arms without strain.
[ ] Length covers most of the seat.
[ ] Sleeves are the right length *or* can be shortened.

### Trousers

[ ] Waistband sits at my natural waist without a belt pulling it up.
[ ] I can sit down comfortably; the waistband does not dig in or slide down at the back.
[ ] Seat: no horizontal pull lines, no pouch of loose cloth.
[ ] Legs: straight, not tight on the thighs.
[ ] Length: can be hemmed (it is fine if they are too long; too short is a problem).

### Shirts

[ ] Collar: one finger fits when buttoned.
[ ] Shoulders: seam at the shoulder bone.
[ ] Tucked in, it stays tucked in when I raise my arms.
[ ] Buttons do not pull across the stomach.
[ ] Cuffs reach the base of the thumb.

### Coats

[ ] Tried on over a jacket or thick sweater.
[ ] Shoulders sit just beyond my jacket shoulders.
[ ] Sleeves cover the jacket cuff.
[ ] Buttons close without strain.
[ ] I can reach forward comfortably.

!TIP Walk around in the shop. Sit on a chair. Bend as if to tie a shoelace. Reach as if for a high shelf. Clothes are for living in, not just for standing still in front of a mirror.

## Warning signs of a bad buy

| W=2.6,3.4 | Warning sign | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| "It will be fine once I lose a few pounds." | It does not fit you. Do not buy it. |
| You need to suck in your stomach to button it | Too small. It will look worse sitting down. |
| The shoulders are wrong but "everything else is great" | Shoulders cannot be fixed easily. Walk away. |
| It only goes with one other thing you own | It will hang unworn. |
| You are buying it mainly because it is reduced | A bargain you never wear is the most expensive item in the wardrobe. |
| The cloth feels thin, shiny or crunchy | It will wrinkle, pill or shine quickly. |
| Seams are wavy, threads hang loose, patterns do not line up at the seams | Poor construction; it will not last. |
| The shop assistant says "everyone's wearing them like that now" | Fashion advice, not fit advice. Trust the checklist. |
| You feel tired and just want to finish | Stop. Come back another day. |

## How to judge quality without knowing brands

You do not need labels to judge quality. Use your hands and eyes.

- **Cloth:** Scrunch a corner in your fist for five seconds and let go. Good wool springs back with few creases. Cheap blends stay crumpled.
- **Weight:** Good trousers and jackets have a little weight and drape. Very light, papery cloth looks cheap quickly.
- **Seams:** Turn it inside out. Seams should be straight, flat and evenly stitched.
- **Pattern matching:** On a check or stripe, look at where pockets meet the body and where sleeves meet the shoulder. On better garments the pattern lines up.
- **Buttons:** Firmly attached, not hanging by loose threads.
- **Lining:** Smooth, not baggy, not pulling. A lining that is too tight makes the jacket pucker.
- **Spare cloth:** On trousers, look inside the back seam. Extra cloth there means the waist can be let out later. A good sign.

## Shopping online

Online shopping can work, but only if you know your measurements and the shop has an easy returns policy.

[ ] Measure a garment that fits you well (lay it flat) and compare with the shop's size chart.
[ ] Read the returns policy before you order. Free, easy returns only.
[ ] Order the size by measurement, not by the size you "always are".
[ ] When it arrives, do the full fitting-room checklist at home, in daylight.
[ ] Keep labels on until you are certain. Return anything that fails the shoulder check.

!WARN Be very careful of social-media advertisements for clothes with suspiciously low prices, countdown timers and no clear address or returns policy. If a deal looks too good to be true, it usually is. Buy from shops you or your family know.

## Sales and discounts

- Make your list *before* the sale. Buy only what is on the list.
- Sales are a good time for basics you already know fit: the same shirts in your size, socks, plain sweaters.
- Avoid buying tailoring (jackets, suits, coats) in a sale unless you can try it on properly and it passes the shoulder check.
- Final-sale, no-returns items should pass every check before you pay.

## The cost-per-wear idea

A simple way to judge value: divide the price by the number of times you will actually wear something.

- A jacket you wear twice a week for five years: hundreds of wears. Even a good price works out to very little per wear.
- A bargain shirt you wear twice before it pills: expensive per wear.

!TRY Next time you are about to buy something, ask: "How many times will I wear this in the next year?" If the honest answer is fewer than ten, put it back unless it is for a special occasion.
