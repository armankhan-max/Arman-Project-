# [Chapter 7] Comfort Without Looking Sloppy

LEAD: Comfort and smartness are not opposites. The trick is to choose comfort that is *built in*, such as soft waistbands, stretch and easy fabrics, rather than comfort that comes from wearing things too big.

## The comfort trap

The most common mistake is to size up for comfort. Larger trousers feel looser for a while, but they sag, the belt pulls them into folds, and the extra cloth looks untidy. A jacket two sizes big is comfortable but looks like a tent.

Instead, keep your correct size and choose garments **designed** to be comfortable.

!WHY Clothes that are too big make a man look smaller and older, not more relaxed. Clothes that fit, made from forgiving fabrics, give the same comfort and a far better look.

## Waistbands that work

| W=1.8,2.6,1.8 | Option | How it works | Looks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Side adjusters | Small buckles or tabs at the side waistband let you tighten or loosen by an inch or two | Smart; no belt needed |
| Hidden elastic panels | A short elastic section inside the side or back of the waistband | Smart; invisible from outside |
| Expanding/flex waistband | Stretch woven into the waistband itself | Smart casual |
| Drawstring inside a flat front | A drawstring hidden inside a normal-looking waistband | Casual |
| Full elastic waist | Visible gathered elastic all round | Very casual: keep for home |
| Braces (suspenders) | Trousers hang from the shoulders, not the waist | Traditional and very comfortable; fit at natural waist |

!TIP Braces are one of the best-kept secrets for men whose waist has changed. The trousers hang straight from the natural waist all day, nothing digs in, and no belt is needed. Choose button-on braces with trousers that have brace buttons, or a tailor can add them inside the waistband.

## Stretch: how much is enough?

Many trousers and jackets now contain a small amount of stretch fibre.

- **A little stretch (about 2–3% of the fabric)** gives easier movement while the garment still looks like normal cloth. This is ideal.
- **A lot of stretch** can look shiny, cling to the body, and lose shape after washing.
- Check the label: a natural fibre (wool or cotton) with a small amount of stretch is the sweet spot.

## Easy fabrics

| W=1.5,2.5,2 | Fabric | Why it is comfortable | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wool flannel | Soft, warm, drapes well, resists creasing | Can be warm in summer |
| Tropical/lightweight wool | Breathes, cool in warm weather, recovers from creases | Choose a darker shade to avoid shine |
| Brushed cotton/chino twill | Soft, easy to wash | Can crease; choose medium weight |
| Jersey (knitted) jackets | Feels like a sweater, looks like a jacket | Choose a structured shoulder |
| Corduroy | Soft, warm, hard-wearing | Wide wale can look bulky |
| Merino wool knits | Soft, light, not itchy, warm | Wash cool, dry flat |
| Linen blends | Cool in heat | Creases; blend with cotton or wool helps |
| Cashmere/lambswool | Very soft, warm | Pills more; needs care |

!NOTE If wool feels scratchy to you, look for merino, lambswool, or a cotton lining in trousers. Wear a cotton undershirt beneath knitwear.

## Comfortable jackets that still look smart

- **Unstructured jackets** have little or no padding in the shoulders and a soft lining. They feel like a cardigan but look like a jacket.
- **Knitted or jersey jackets** stretch with movement and do not crease when you sit in a car.
- **Half-lined jackets** are cooler and lighter.
- Keep the same fit rules: the shoulder must still end at your shoulder bone.

## Easier dressing

For men with stiff hands, sore shoulders or limited reach, small changes help a lot. (If dressing is difficult because of a health condition, ask your doctor or an occupational therapist about aids designed for this.)

- **Shirts:** choose larger buttons, or ask a tailor to replace small buttons with slightly larger ones. Keep cuffs buttoned and slip shirts on over the head if easier.
- **Front-fastening knitwear:** cardigans and zip-neck sweaters are easier than pullovers.
- **Shoes:** slip-on leather shoes, or lace-ups with elastic laces, look identical to normal shoes.
- **Ties:** a ready-knotted tie on a hidden clip is acceptable for anyone who finds knotting difficult; choose a good fabric so it does not look obviously pre-tied.
- **Zips:** a small loop of ribbon or a key ring through a zip pull makes it easier to grip.
- **Trousers:** side adjusters avoid fiddling with belt buckles.

## Smart vs sloppy: the small details

| W=3,3 | Sloppy | Smart |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirt with a stretched, wavy neckline | T-shirt with a firm neckline, or a polo/knit shirt |
| Elastic-waist trousers worn out of the house | Trousers with hidden elastic or side adjusters |
| Fleece top with logos | Plain merino or cotton sweater |
| Track trousers outside the gym | Chinos with a small amount of stretch |
| Running shoes with smart trousers | Clean leather sneakers or slip-on leather shoes |
| Shirt untucked and wrinkled | Shirt tucked, or a casual shirt cut to be worn untucked (hem just below the belt) |
| Cardigan with stretched pockets and loose buttons | Cardigan with firm buttons and empty pockets |

!TRY Check the waistband of every pair of trousers you own. Anything that digs in, slides down, or needs the belt pulled hard is a candidate for the tailor (side adjusters, hidden elastic or brace buttons), or for replacement with a better-designed waist.
