# [Chapter 4] The Closet Clean-Out

LEAD: A full wardrobe with nothing to wear is the most common complaint of all. The problem is rarely too few clothes. It is too many of the wrong ones, crowding out the good ones.

## Why this is harder than it sounds

Clothes carry memories. The suit from a son's wedding, the jacket bought for a retirement dinner, the sweater a late wife knitted or chose. Some men also grew up in households where nothing was thrown away. These feelings are real and deserve respect.

The method in this chapter does not ask you to throw away your memories. It asks you to separate **clothes you wear** from **clothes you keep**. Both are allowed. They just should not share the same rail.

!TIP Make a small "memory box" or a separate section at the end of the wardrobe for sentimental pieces you will not wear. They stay safe, and your everyday rail becomes clear.

## What you need

- Two to three hours, or three sessions of one hour (one category per session)
- A bed or table to lay things out on
- Three labelled boxes or bags: **KEEP**, **FIX**, **REMOVE**
- A full-length mirror and good daylight
- Optional but recommended: a trusted second opinion (wife, daughter, son, friend)

## The method: Keep, Fix or Remove

Work by category, not by shelf: all trousers first, then all jackets, then shirts, then knitwear, then coats, then shoes. Seeing all your trousers together shows instantly how many navy chinos you actually own.

For each item, ask the five questions below, in order. Stop at the first clear answer.

| W=0.4,3,2.6 | # | Question | If the answer is... |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Have I worn it in the last 12 months (allowing for season)? | No: lean towards REMOVE |
| 2 | Does it pass the fit checks in Chapter 1? | Yes: KEEP. No: go to question 3 |
| 3 | Can a tailor fix it with an *easy or moderate* alteration (Chapter 2)? | Yes: FIX. No: REMOVE |
| 4 | Is the cloth in good condition: no shine, holes, moth damage, fading? | No: REMOVE (unless it's a memory piece) |
| 5 | Does it flatter my face and go with at least two other things I own (Chapter 3)? | No: REMOVE |

### The KEEP pile

Fits now, in good condition, flatters you, goes with other things. Hang it back up properly (see Chapter 9 for hangers).

### The FIX pile

Good cloth, good shoulders, flattering colour, but needs a hem, sleeves, waist adjustment, buttons or a small repair. Put a note on each item with what it needs. Take no more than three items to the tailor at a time.

!WARN The FIX pile has a time limit. If something has been in the FIX pile for three months and you have not taken it to a tailor, it moves to REMOVE. Otherwise the FIX pile becomes a second cluttered wardrobe.

### The REMOVE pile

Does not fit and cannot be fixed easily, damaged, unflattering, or simply never worn. Options:

- Donate clean, wearable items to a charity shop or clothing bank
- Offer good suits and coats to a charity that helps people dress for job interviews
- Recycle damaged textiles at a textile recycling point
- Pass a well-loved piece to a grandson or nephew who might wear it

## Common sticking points, and how to decide

| W=2.3,3.7 | You say... | Ask yourself... |
|---|---|---|
| "It was expensive." | The money is already spent. Will wearing a badly-fitting expensive jacket make it worth more? |
| "I might lose weight." | Keep one or two such items in a box, not the whole rail. Fit the man you are today. |
| "I might need it one day." | When did "one day" last happen? Would you buy this today if you saw it in a shop? |
| "It's still perfectly good." | Good for someone. Is it good for *you*? Donate it so it gets worn. |
| "My wife bought it." | Would she want you to wear something that does not suit you, or something that does? |
| "I wore it to an important day." | It is a memory piece. Box it with care. It does not need daily rail space. |

## Your 20-piece capsule wardrobe

A *capsule wardrobe* is a small set of clothes that all go together. Twenty well-chosen pieces give you well over a hundred combinations, and dressing takes two minutes.

This list is a template. Adjust for your climate and lifestyle. Most men find they already own half of it.

## Arranging the wardrobe

- Hang by category: jackets, then trousers, then shirts, so you can see choices at a glance.
- Within each category, hang from dark to light.
- Put the KEEP pieces you wear most at eye level and within easy reach.
- Fold knitwear; never hang it (see Chapter 9).
- Leave space between hangers. Crushed clothes crease and you cannot see what you own.
