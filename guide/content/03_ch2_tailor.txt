# [Chapter 2] Tailor Talk

LEAD: A tailor is the best-value person in your wardrobe. This chapter takes away the guesswork: what they can fix, what they cannot, what to say, what to ask, and how to check the work before you pay.

## Why men avoid the tailor (and why they shouldn't)

Many men over 60 have never set foot in an alterations shop, or went once for a wedding suit decades ago. The reasons are usually the same: not knowing what to ask, worrying it will be expensive, not wanting to be "fussed over", or feeling it is something for wealthy men.

Here is the truth. Most of the alterations that make the biggest difference are **routine, quick jobs** that a local alterations shop does dozens of times a week. Hemming trousers and shortening sleeves are the bread and butter of the trade. You are not asking for anything unusual.

!WHY A modest jacket with three simple alterations will usually look better than an expensive jacket worn straight off the rack. Alterations are not a luxury. They are the step that ready-made clothes were always designed to need.

## Choosing a tailor

You do not need a famous name. You need someone careful.

- Ask friends, neighbours, a dry-cleaner, or a men's shop which alterations person they trust.
- Look for a shop where garments are hanging with tickets and pins: a sign of steady work.
- A good tailor will ask you to put the garment on and will pin it on your body. Be wary of anyone who only measures the garment flat on the table for anything beyond a simple hem.
- They should explain what they will do and give you a price and a pickup date before starting.
- Start small: bring one pair of trousers to hem. If you like the result, bring the jacket next.

## The alterations menu

This table shows the common alterations, what each one fixes, and how difficult (and therefore how costly, relative to the others) it usually is.

| W=1.8,2.4,1,1.6 | Alteration | What it fixes | Difficulty | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hem trousers | Trousers too long, pooling on shoes | Easy | Always |
| Taper trouser legs | Wide, flappy legs | Easy-moderate | Usually |
| Take in trouser waist | Waist too big, belt bunching | Moderate | Usually |
| Let out trouser waist | Waist too tight (only if spare cloth) | Moderate | If cloth allows |
| Shorten jacket sleeves (plain cuff) | Sleeves too long | Easy-moderate | Always |
| Shorten sleeves (working buttons) | Sleeves too long on buttoned cuffs | Harder | On good jackets |
| Take in jacket sides | Boxy, loose middle | Moderate | Usually |
| Let out jacket sides | X-pull at button (if spare cloth) | Moderate | If cloth allows |
| Fix collar gap | Gap at back of neck | Harder | Ask the tailor |
| Shorten jacket length | Jacket too long | Hard, risky | Rarely |
| Shorten shirt sleeves | Cuffs past the hand | Moderate | On good shirts |
| Take in shirt sides | Billowing shirt body | Easy-moderate | Often |
| Shorten coat sleeves | Coat sleeves to fingertips | Moderate | Usually |
| Replace buttons | Cheap or missing buttons | Easy | Always |
| Re-line a jacket | Torn lining | Moderate-hard | On jackets you love |

!TIP Bring the shoes you will wear when trousers are being hemmed, and wear a shirt and the kind of sweater you normally wear under the jacket when sleeves or sides are being fitted. The tailor fits what you bring.

## What can and cannot be changed

### Usually fixable

- Trouser length, leg width, waist (within limits)
- Sleeve length on jackets, coats and shirts
- Jacket and shirt sides (taking in)
- Buttons, linings, small tears, loose seams
- Moderate collar gaps

### Difficult, risky or not worth it

- **Jacket shoulders** (too wide or too narrow)
- **Shirt collar size**
- Jacket length, by more than a small amount (pocket positions and proportions go wrong)
- Making a jacket much bigger (only possible if there is spare cloth in the seams)
- Fixing badly worn, shiny or moth-eaten cloth

!WARN If a garment needs three or more "difficult" alterations, it is almost always cheaper and better to replace it. Do not throw good money after a bad fit.

## Before you go: the three-minute preparation

1. **Wear the outfit.** Put on the garment with the right shoes, shirt and belt.
2. **Look in the mirror** and use the Quick Start scorecard. Note what bothers you.
3. **Write a short list.** Take the *Show This to Your Tailor* sheet at the back of this guide.
4. **Empty all pockets.** A phone or wallet changes how a jacket hangs.
5. **Know your limit.** Decide what you are willing to spend on this garment before you go.

## Scripts: what to say

Many men feel awkward because they do not know the words. These scripts do the work for you. Read them aloud once at home; it helps.

### Opening the conversation

!SAY I'd like this to fit me properly. Could you have a look at the trousers and jacket while I'm wearing them and tell me what you would change?

### Trousers

!SAY Could you hem these so the front just touches my shoes, with a slight break? These are the shoes I'll wear with them.

!SAY The waist slips down under my stomach. Is there enough cloth to adjust the waist so they sit higher and stay there?

### Jacket sleeves

!SAY I'd like to see about a quarter to half an inch of shirt cuff. Could you check both arms separately?

### The middle of the jacket

!SAY It feels boxy around the middle. Could you take in the sides a little so it follows my shape, but keep it comfortable to sit in?

### When you are unsure

!SAY Honestly, is this jacket worth altering, or would you put the money towards a different one?

!NOTE A good tailor will answer that last question honestly. If they always say "yes, we can fix everything", be cautious.

## Questions to ask before they start

[ ] What exactly will you change, and how will it look afterwards?
[ ] How much will it cost in total?
[ ] When will it be ready?
[ ] Will I need a second fitting?
[ ] Is there enough spare cloth if I gain or lose a little weight later?
[ ] For sleeves: will the buttons or the vent be affected?
[ ] For trousers: will you keep the original hem style (plain or turn-up/cuffed)?

## At the fitting: what to do

- Stand naturally. Do not pull your stomach in or stand to attention. You will not stand that way at a dinner.
- Sit down in the pinned trousers. Lift your arms in the pinned jacket. Comfort matters.
- Look in the mirror from the side, not just the front.
- If something feels tight now, it will feel tighter after lunch. Say so.

## The pickup checklist

Never pay and leave without trying the garment on. Check:

[ ] Trousers: front hem just touches the shoe; back hem does not drag
[ ] Both trouser legs are the same length
[ ] Hem stitching is invisible from the outside
[ ] Jacket sleeves show the shirt cuff on both arms
[ ] Sleeve buttons are evenly spaced and the same distance from the edge on both sides
[ ] Jacket front closes smoothly; no new pulling
[ ] Sitting down is comfortable
[ ] Pressing is clean: no shiny marks from the iron, no chalk lines left
[ ] Any pockets that were tacked shut have been opened, if you asked

!TIP If something is not right, say so politely and calmly at pickup. Good tailors expect small adjustments and would rather fix them than have an unhappy customer. "Could you take another look at the left sleeve? It seems a touch longer than the right."

## A smart order of work

If you have a full wardrobe that needs attention, do not take it all at once. Prioritise like this:

1. The trousers you wear most (hemming and waist)
2. Your most-worn jacket (sleeves, then sides)
3. Your coat (sleeves)
4. Your best suit, before the next wedding or funeral, not the week of it
5. Good shirts you wear often (sleeves, sides)

!TRY This week, pick the one pair of trousers you wear most and take them for hemming. It is the cheapest, fastest alteration, and it will show you how much difference a tailor makes.
