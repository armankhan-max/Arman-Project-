# [Quick Start] The 5-Minute Mirror Test

LEAD: Do this before you read anything else. It shows you where your money and effort should go first.

## What you need

- A full-length mirror (or a phone propped on a shelf, using the timer)
- Your most-worn jacket, a pair of everyday trousers, a collared shirt and the shoes you wear most
- Good light: daylight from a window is best. Overhead light hides wrinkles and exaggerates shadows.
- A pencil and the scorecard below

!TIP Stand the way you normally stand, not the way you stand for photographs. A tailor fits the man who walks out of the shop, not the man posing in the mirror. Breathe out, let your shoulders settle, and then look.

## The test, minute by minute

### Minute 1: Shoulders and collar (jacket on, buttoned)

1. Look at where the shoulder seam ends. It should end where your own shoulder bone ends.
2. Look for a dent or ripple just below the shoulder, at the top of the sleeve. A dent means the shoulder is too wide.
3. Turn sideways. The jacket collar should sit flat against your shirt collar at the back of the neck, with no gap.

### Minute 2: Sleeves and cuffs (arms hanging naturally)

1. Let your arms hang. The jacket sleeve should stop around the wrist bone.
2. You should see roughly a quarter to half an inch of shirt cuff.
3. If the sleeve touches your knuckles or hides all the shirt cuff, it is too long.

### Minute 3: The middle (still buttoned)

1. Look at the button. Do you see an X-shaped pull radiating from it? Too tight.
2. Does the front hang open or flap away from your body at the sides? Too loose.
3. Does the jacket cover your seat at the back? For most men it should, or nearly.

### Minute 4: Trousers

1. Where does the waistband sit? It should sit at your natural waist, not under the stomach.
2. Look at the hem from the front. It should rest lightly on the shoe with at most one soft fold.
3. Turn around. Is there a lot of loose cloth hanging under the seat? That is a sign the trousers are sitting too low or are too big.

### Minute 5: Shirt collar, shoes and overall

1. Button the top shirt button. Can you slide one finger in comfortably? Good. None or three? Wrong size.
2. Look down at the shoes. Are the heels worn on one side? Is the leather grey and scuffed?
3. Finally, step back and ask: *does anything look borrowed?* Trust your first instinct.

## Your scorecard

Tick each line that is a problem. Each tick is a job, not a failure.

| W=3,1,1.6 | Check | Problem? | Fixable by a tailor? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoulder seam past the shoulder bone, or a dent below it | ☐ | Rarely. Mostly a *buy differently* issue |
| Collar gap at the back of the neck | ☐ | Often, ask |
| Sleeve too long (no shirt cuff showing) | ☐ | Yes, usually easy |
| Sleeve too short (all cuff, wrist bone showing) | ☐ | Sometimes, if spare cloth |
| X-pull at the button | ☐ | Sometimes (let out) |
| Front hangs loose, boxy | ☐ | Yes, take in sides |
| Trousers sit under the stomach | ☐ | Partly. Waist fitting helps |
| Trousers pile up on shoes | ☐ | Yes, very easy |
| Shirt collar too tight or gaping | ☐ | No. Buy the right size |
| Shoes worn down or dull | ☐ | Cobbler and polish |

!TRY Circle your three biggest ticks. Those three fixes will change how you look more than anything you could buy. Write them on the inside cover of this guide or on your phone.

## The top 10 fit mistakes, at a glance

These are the mistakes that walk through a fitting-room door most often. Each one has its own section later in the guide.

| W=0.5,3,3.4 | # | The mistake | The quick fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | Trousers too long, bunching on the shoe | Hem so the front just touches the shoe |
| 9 | Jacket sleeves too long | Shorten to the wrist bone, show a little shirt cuff |
| 8 | Trousers sitting too low under the stomach | Wear at the natural waist, choose a higher rise |
| 7 | Black worn next to the face | Swap to navy, charcoal, brown or soft tones |
| 6 | Shirt collar the wrong size | Re-measure, buy the size that fits today |
| 5 | Stuffed jacket pockets, and stitched-shut pockets | Empty them, snip the tacking threads |
| 4 | Worn-down, dull shoes | Re-heel and polish |
| 3 | Tie the wrong length or width | Tip at the belt buckle; width close to the lapel |
| 2 | Tired, pilled, stretched sweaters | De-pill, wash cool, dry flat, or retire |
| 1 | An off-the-rack jacket that was never altered | Buy for the shoulders, let a tailor do the rest |

### Why these ten?

Because they share three things. They are **visible from across a room**. They are **cheap or free to fix**. And they are **invisible to the man wearing them**, because he sees himself every day and stops noticing. The people around him notice at once.

!WHY Studies of first impressions consistently find that people form a judgement about someone within seconds, and clothing is a big part of what they see. You do not need to dress to impress strangers, but you deserve to be seen as you are: capable, careful and put-together. Poor fit quietly sends the opposite message.

## Why men stop noticing fit

This is worth understanding, because it explains why the fixes in this guide get put off for years.

- **Habit.** We buy the same size we bought fifteen years ago, because it worked then.
- **The mirror lies kindly.** We look at ourselves from the front, face-on, for a second or two. Fit problems show up from the side and the back.
- **Nobody tells us.** Friends are polite. Family members do not want to hurt feelings. Shop assistants want the sale.
- **"It's my age."** Many men decide that clothes simply do not suit them any more. Almost always, it is not the body that is the problem. It is the clothes that were never adjusted to it.
- **Tailors feel intimidating.** Many men have never been to one and do not know what to ask. Chapter 2 solves that completely.

!NOTE If you take only one idea from this guide, take this one: **it is not you, it is the fit.** Clothes are cut for an average body. Every real body needs small adjustments. That has always been true, at every age.
