# [Chapter 10] For Family: Buying Clothes for Him

LEAD: Written for wives, partners, daughters, sons and grandchildren. How to buy him clothes he will actually wear, how to get his sizes without a fuss, and how to help without hurting his pride.

## First, the human side

Many men over 60 have strong feelings about clothes, even if they rarely talk about them. Some feel they no longer matter. Some feel their bodies have let them down. Some are simply attached to what they know. A gift of clothing can feel like a compliment or a criticism, depending on how it is offered.

### What tends to work

- **Talk about the clothes, not his body.** "This jacket's cut is too boxy" lands better than "you've put on weight".
- **Praise what already works.** "You always look good in navy" makes him more open to another navy piece.
- **Offer choice.** Two options to choose from feel like a decision; one gift can feel like an instruction.
- **Make it about an occasion.** "Something new for the wedding" gives a reason that is not about how he looks every day.
- **Keep receipts and say so.** "If it's not right, we'll change it together" removes pressure.

### What tends to backfire

- Buying something much more fashionable than he would choose for himself
- Comments in front of others about his clothes
- Throwing out his old favourites without asking
- Buying a size smaller "as motivation"
- Choosing on brand or price instead of fit and colour

!WHY Most people resist change that feels imposed and accept change they feel they chose. Ask, suggest and offer, and let him decide. A man who feels respected will try new things; a man who feels criticised will hold on to his old cardigan forever.

## How to get his sizes without asking

- **Look at labels** on clothes he wears often *and* that fit him well. (Not the ones that fit badly.)
- **Measure a garment that fits** while it lies flat: chest across from armpit to armpit, waist across the top, trouser inside leg, sleeve from shoulder seam to cuff.
- **Collar size:** check the label inside his best-fitting shirt collar.
- **Shoe size:** inside the tongue or on the sole of shoes he finds comfortable.
- **Better still:** fill in the size sheet below with him. Many men enjoy a reason to be properly measured.

### How to measure (with his help)

- **Chest:** tape under the arms, around the fullest part of the chest, snug but not tight. Arms relaxed.
- **Natural waist:** tape around the body at belly-button level. Let him breathe normally.
- **Inside leg:** from the crotch seam to the floor on a pair of trousers that are the right length, laid flat.
- **Collar:** tape around the base of the neck where the collar sits, with one finger under the tape.
- **Sleeve:** from the centre back of the neck, over the shoulder, down to the wrist bone, with the arm slightly bent.

## Safe gifts: almost always right

| W=2.2,3.8 | Gift | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Pale blue shirt in his exact collar size | Flatters silver hair; easy to wear |
| Navy or grey merino sweater | Warm, soft, goes with everything |
| Good wool scarf in navy, grey or burgundy | One size fits all |
| Leather gloves in his glove size | Practical and smart |
| Cedar shoe trees in his shoe size | He will use them every day |
| A shoe-care kit (brushes, polish, cloth) | A ritual many men enjoy |
| A clothes brush | Small, useful, lasts decades |
| Quality socks in navy, grey and brown | Always needed |
| A gift voucher *for a tailor* | Turns clothes he already owns into clothes that fit |
| Wooden hangers | Makes the whole wardrobe look better |

## Riskier gifts: check first

| W=2.2,3.8 | Gift | Why it is risky |
|---|---|---|
| Jackets and suits | Must be tried on for shoulder fit |
| Trousers | Waist and rise preferences are very personal |
| Shoes | Fit and comfort are individual |
| Anything in a bold colour or pattern | May never leave the drawer |
| Very fashionable cuts (slim, cropped, short jackets) | Rarely flattering after 60 |
| Novelty ties and socks | Fun for one day, then forgotten |

!TIP The best clothing "gift" might be a shopping trip together. Offer to go with him, take this guide, and buy one thing that passes all the checks. Make it a morning out with coffee or lunch afterwards.

## Helping him with the closet clean-out

- Choose a calm day, not before a stressful event.
- Ask him to lead. Your job is to hold up the mirror (literally) and ask the five questions in Chapter 4.
- Let him keep memory pieces. Offer a box for them.
- Celebrate the KEEP pile: "Look how many good things you have."
- Take the REMOVE bags away the same day, so they do not drift back into the wardrobe.

## If he resists

- Start with something that is purely practical, such as hemming trousers that drag on the ground or replacing worn-out shoes.
- Use a reason he values: comfort, saving money, a special occasion, the grandchildren's photos.
- Let him see the result. Once a man sees himself in trousers that fit properly, he usually asks for the next alteration himself.
