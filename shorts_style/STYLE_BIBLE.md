## 1. The script: rules taken from the transcripts

**Length:** 220–230 words for a 60–65s short. **Pace is 3.6–3.8 words per second**, which is fast. (The earlier estimate of "3 w/s" was wrong. A script written at 3 w/s comes out ~20% too long.)

**Tense and voice:** third person, present tense, casual and dry. The narrator tells it like gossip: "the guy next door decides it's probably the wind."

### Beat structure

| Time | Beat | Device | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **Hook = the strangest fact, stated flatly.** Start in the middle. | Plain statement, no question | "They buried a man alive with a bell tied to his finger." / "They painted the jail cell pink." / "Look at these two scratch cards." |
| 2–12s | **The rule of this world** | **"You see, …"** | "You see, people were so scared of waking up in a coffin…" / "You see, memories are money here." |
| ~12–35s | **Escalation with precise, funny numbers** | Exact numbers make it believable and funny | 1 pint into 1 gallon, exactly 15 minutes, 300 lb of tattoos, 2 seconds, 2:00 a.m., "Three short, one long", 300 trips to the Bahamas, 90% |
| along the way | **Short deadpan quotes from side characters** | One line each, no tags | "You go check." "No, you go check." / "Bodies twitch. He's dead." / "Barbie's going to love it in here." / "Sir, that's impossible." "Okay." |
| ~35s | **The narrator asks the viewer's question out loud** | **"Wait, ___? Well, …"** / "So what does he do?" | "Wait, practiced? Well, he was never dead." / "Wait, what? Well, nobody can explain it." |
| 35–55s | **A second escalation or turn** | "Until one night…", "Instead…", "But that same week…" | Guard forgets the inmate; the wife just sits there |
| last 5s | **Payoff with a twist of meaning** | Often a second **"You see, …"**, then a cold last line | "every debt got buried right along with him. And the house was all hers." / "it only works for 15 minutes." / "Jerry has no idea who this kid is." |

### Tone
Not just comedy. The comedy is **deadpan irony**, and endings are often **dark or bittersweet** (Grave Bell, Memory Shop). The jokes come from understatement, never from slapstick in the narration.

### Endings
- Ending on the last word is enough: no outro, no "subscribe".
- Some endings **loop back to the first shot**. Pink Prison: "…does the exact opposite" plays over the rage shot from the start. Lottery: the script cuts off mid-sentence with "from every… store". Burglar: the burglar sits on the same couch as in the opening, with the cat.

## 2. Visual format
- 9:16, 30fps. The first frame already shows the story. No logo.
- **The characters are 2D stick figures in a fully 3D-lit set.**
  - Characters: round white head, thick black outline, bean-shaped body, thin line legs, simple expressive faces.
  - Sets: real perspective, soft shadows, light rays through windows, depth.
- **Captions:** 1–2 words at a time, white bold sans with a black outline, centred mid-screen, synced word by word.
- **Each location has one colour.** Night is deep blue with a warm lamp. The climax colour takes over the frame (pink cell). Colour temperature shows the mood changing.

## 3. Camera grammar

**Almost no hard cuts.** Measured: 1 in Burglar, about 4 in Japan.

Scene changes use:
1. **The camera passes through something.** It follows the character through a window or door.
2. **A POV transition**, such as a fisheye peephole with a round vignette.
3. **The camera drops to floor detail and rises again**, e.g. couch leg, slippers, ruler.
4. **A drone pull-out**: from the scene up to an aerial view, then down into the next place.
5. **An object wipe**: a phone, card or ID passes across the lens.
6. **A cross-dissolve during motion**, so it never sits static.

How the camera moves within a shot:
- **It never stops moving.** A slow push-in of 5–10% runs under every sentence.
- **Snap punch-in on the punchline or reaction word**, about 100%→130% in a few frames.
- **Rage or chaos:** push in, shake, red vein and crack effects, a floor crack under the feet.
- **Fisheye or tilted wide shots** when the world has gone wrong.
- **Angle carries meaning:**
  - Low angle means power (cop, boss).
  - Top-down or high angle means helplessness (couple in bed, man in the corner).
- **Macro inserts on props carry the information:** ruler, paint can, number grids, stopwatch, FIRE button, empty inbox.

## 4. Acting and gags
- Small face changes: side-eye, one brow raised, gritted-teeth grimace, a sweat drop, a closed-eye smile.
- **Hold on a deadpan face** after a dumb line. The face is the joke.
- Gags in the props: a "DON'T MOVE" fridge note, a "DESK STUFF" box, a cobweb in the inbox, a cat as silent witness.
- Characters pop in with squash and stretch. Effects: sparkles for "winner", paper bursts, rainbow highlights on numbers.

## 5. Sound

**Measured:**
- Loudness stays flat around −16 dB the whole way.
- Audio starts at 0.1s.
- Something hits every 0.4–0.6s.

**Silence is used only as a comedic beat.** In all four videos the only full drops (about 0.1s) come right before a punchline or turn:
- Japan 13.6s: "Your new job is… nothing."
- Japan 40.1s: "Then one Monday…"
- Lottery 50.3s: "That's impossible."

**BGM:** a low bed under a dominant voice. The Memory Shop transcript marks `[music]` exactly on the emotional beats (rent, "his head is mostly bad days", "Kid, you sell this", "cured", "every happy year"). So **the music swells on emotional words and stays down everywhere else.**

**SFX (inferred from where the hits fall, not confirmed by ear):**
- a whoosh on every camera move
- a pop when a character appears
- tick, click or thud on props

Genre and instruments are not confirmed. Ask Arman, or analyse the mp3 by ear-equivalent tools before claiming.

## 6. Deliverable format when Arman gives a topic
1. A script of 220–230 words with the beat structure above. Mark the "You see," and "Wait, ___? Well," beats and the silence beats.
2. A shot table with one row per caption chunk or camera move. Columns:
   - time
   - VO
   - caption
   - set and acting
   - camera (move, start→end framing, easing)
   - effects
   - SFX
   - BGM (bed, swell, duck, or silence)
3. The transition used into every new location (from the list in section 3).
4. If asked, render it with the code pipeline already in this repo (`video/`, `blackhole/`: skia drawing, ffmpeg render, synthesized audio). **Rendering is possible here.** It is not limited to writing prompts.

## 7. Mistakes made earlier; don't repeat them
1. **Pace was guessed at "3 words/sec".** The real pace is 3.6–3.8. Calculate it from the transcripts.
2. **Dialogue was quoted as exact when it was rebuilt from 1–2-word captions** (the cop line in Burglar). Mark anything taken from captions as approximate.
3. **The tone was called "funny".** It is deadpan irony, often with a dark or bittersweet ending.
4. **Key script devices were missed:** the double "You see,", the "Wait, ___? Well," beat, exact numbers, and two-line deadpan exchanges.
5. **Every video was said to loop.** Only some loop; others end on a cold last line.
6. **It was said that rendering is impossible.** This repo already renders 2D videos from code.
7. **Matching which transcript belongs to which video was not checked against the files.** Always map transcript ↔ media and say which ones are missing.
