A man sold the Eiffel Tower. Then he came back and sold it again. You see, in 1925, the tower was only supposed to be temporary. It was rusting, it cost a fortune to repaint, and the newspapers were asking if Paris should just tear it down. So a con man named Victor Lustig prints fake government letterheads, books a suite at the fanciest hotel in Paris, and invites six scrap metal dealers to a secret meeting. He tells them the city is quietly scrapping the tower. Seven thousand tons of iron. Highest bid wins. And please, tell no one. The public would riot. One dealer, André Poisson, takes the bait, but he gets suspicious. Why is a government official in such a hurry? So Lustig leans in and asks him for a bribe. Wait, a bribe? Well, that's what convinced him. Only a real government official would be that corrupt. Poisson pays for the tower, pays the bribe, and Lustig is on a train out of France with a suitcase full of cash. Then he checks the papers every single day. Nothing. Not one word. Because Poisson would rather lose everything than tell Paris he bought the Eiffel Tower. So Lustig comes back, invites six new dealers, and gives the exact same speech. This time, the buyer goes to the police. And Lustig? He's already on a boat to America.
